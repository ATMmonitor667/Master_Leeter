MASTER LEETER — END-TO-END UI REDESIGN PLAN
Branch: UI
Written: 2026-10-04
Base: main @ 731eed2 (apps/web, Next.js 15 App Router + React 19, plain CSS)
Status: design and implementation specification. No product code changed by
        this document. Every number below is a decision, not a placeholder.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. HOW TO READ THIS, AND WHAT IT MAY NOT CHANGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is a redesign of the whole surface: brand, tokens, components, every
screen, every state, the motion language, the accessibility contract, the
performance budget, and the order in which to ship it.

Read sections 1-4 before touching a file. Sections 5-7 are the build. Sections
8-10 are the quality bars that decide whether the build is done. Section 11 is
the shipping order; it is written so that every phase is independently
mergeable and independently revertible.

PRODUCT INVARIANTS — the redesign is wrong if it breaks any of these. They come
from CLAUDE.md and from the interview design, not from taste:

  I1. The full problem statement is never rendered as text. Oral delivery only.
      Captions are an accessibility feature for the CANDIDATE's own speech, not
      a reading path for the interviewer's words.
  I2. No chat transcript of the interviewer, no avatar, no face. The entire
      interviewer presence is a state and a few words.
  I3. Silence is the normal state. The UI must never fill it with chatter,
      tips, nudges, streaks, confetti, or progress gamification.
  I4. The live interviewer never grades. Nothing score-shaped may appear on the
      interview screen — not a running meter, not a "confidence" bar.
  I5. No AI autocomplete in the editor. The editor is deliberately unhelpful.
  I6. Nothing about the gate's internal state is exposed ("considering a
      probe"), or candidates will play the system instead of the interview.
  I7. The timer is server-authoritative and visually calm: no colour shifts, no
      pulsing, no urgency theatre.
  I8. Privacy posture stays visible and honest: no raw-audio retention claims,
      no dark patterns around deletion, no pre-ticked consent.

Everything else is open, including the colour system, the layout, the type, the
component architecture, the stylesheet strategy and the navigation model.

1. AUDIT OF THE CURRENT UI (what we are actually starting from)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Inventory, as of this branch point:

  Routes      /, /login, /interview/[sessionId], /report/[sessionId],
              /history, /settings, /faq, /privacy, /support, robots, sitemap,
              opengraph-image
  Components  14 product components + 2 decorative canvas components
  Styling     ONE file: apps/web/app/globals.css, 490 lines, ~42 KB
  Tooling     No Tailwind, no CSS modules, no component library, no Storybook,
              no visual regression, no a11y automation
  Deps        next 15, react 19, @monaco-editor/react, @supabase/supabase-js

The work that exists is better than it looks: the component comments carry real
product reasoning, the accessibility instincts are present (skip link, roles,
aria-live, reduced-motion block, focus trap hook), and the information
architecture of the interview screen is sound. What is missing is a SYSTEM.
Below is what breaks as a result.

1.1 ARCHITECTURE: one stylesheet, written in layers of time, not layers of CSS

  * globals.css contains at least three design passes stacked on top of each
    other ("2026 cockpit pass", "Landing", "Interview workspace", "Report").
    The later block silently overrides the earlier one.
  * Twenty selectors are declared more than once in the same file, so the
    later pass silently reassigns the earlier one: .workspace-header (x3,
    counting its media-query copies), and x2 each for .timer, .setup-card,
    .scenario-card, .report-meta, .panel-label, .panel-label::before,
    .landing-shell, .landing-nav, .landing-hero, .landing-copy h1,
    .landing-copy h1 span, .interviewer-status, .eyebrow, .drill-grid,
    .dimension-card, .danger-button, .brand-mark, .stage-progress,
    .workspace-header-right.
  * At least one of those collisions is dead design: `.landing-copy h1 span` is
    given a gradient-clipped text treatment in the cockpit block and then
    reassigned `color: var(--muted)` by the later Landing block. The gradient
    has never shipped. Nobody noticed, which is the point.
  * There is no @layer, no import graph, no file-per-concern. Every new screen
    appends to the bottom and hopes.

1.2 TYPOGRAPHY: the typeface is declared and never loaded, and the scale is tiny

  * `--sans: Inter, ui-sans-serif, ...` — but there is no next/font, no
    @font-face, and the CSP allows `font-src 'self' data:`. Inter is not in the
    bundle. Every design decision was made in Inter and every user sees Segoe
    UI (Windows) or SF (macOS). Letter-spacing values tuned for Inter
    (-0.065em on the hero) are actively wrong for the fallbacks.
  * Base `body { font-size: 14px }`, and the stylesheet declares ~70 font sizes
    below 13px: 7px x1, 8px x3, 9px x9, 10px x10, 11px x21, 12px x26. The timer
    caption, the interviewer label and the stage rail are 8-9px uppercase mono.
    That is below any reasonable floor, and it is the text people read while
    under interview stress.
  * Inputs inherit 14px, which triggers iOS Safari zoom-on-focus (needs 16px).
  * No tabular figures anywhere, so the countdown reflows every second.

1.3 COLOUR: a fixed dark theme with hand-mixed hexes

  * `color-scheme: dark` with no light theme at all. A 9am interview in a bright
    room is the common case, and dark-only is a real accessibility exclusion
    (astigmatism, photophobia both directions).
  * ~20 raw hex variables with no ramps, so every new state invents a value:
    #5f6a7b, #596576, #4e596b, #303b4c appear as one-off literals.
  * Contrast risks: `.stage-step` at #5f6a7b on #07080c is ~4.0:1 — under the
    4.5:1 floor for text that small. `.console-code b` is ~3.0:1 (decorative,
    aria-hidden, acceptable, but it sets the house style).
  * Status colour is the only signal for several states (connection dot,
    caption state, history status). Colour alone is not an accessible signal.
  * `.workspace-loading` dims the whole grid with opacity .55 + saturate(.6),
    which also dims the text under it below contrast minimums.

1.4 LAYOUT: viewport units and two breakpoints

  * `height: 100vh` on the workspace (9 uses of 100vh in the file). On mobile
    browsers with dynamic chrome this clips the editor and the header.
  * Breakpoints at 900px and 620px only, both global. The side panes do not
    respond to their own width — a notepad at 330px and at 700px get the same
    type and padding. No container queries.
  * The interview grid is fixed at 1.75fr / .85fr. It cannot be resized by the
    candidate, who may want the editor full-width while thinking.

1.5 ACCESSIBILITY: good instincts, incomplete contract

  * `a:focus-visible` has no focus style — the global rule covers inputs,
    textarea, select and button only. Links in a dark UI get the UA default.
  * The sticky, blurred `.workspace-header` can cover a focused control when
    the page scrolls (WCAG 2.2 SC 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured).
  * Target sizes: several icon-only ghost buttons compute to ~22-28px. SC 2.5.8
    wants 24x24 CSS px minimum with spacing exceptions. Untested today.
  * Two `aria-live="polite"` regions can fire together (InterviewerStatus and
    SpeechCaption). Overlapping live regions produce either double speech or
    dropped announcements, depending on the screen reader.
  * `aria-label` is applied to non-interactive `div`s (`.hero-console`,
    `.workspace-grid` sections). On a generic element the label is not exposed.
  * The root layout wraps everything in `<div id="main-content">`, and each page
    then renders its own `<main>`; the skip link therefore targets a generic
    div, not the main landmark.
  * No forced-colors (Windows High Contrast) handling; backgrounds are painted
    with gradients that disappear there, leaving borderless panels.
  * No visible-focus audit, no 400% zoom audit, no keyboard map documented.

1.6 MOTION: decorative loops that never stop

  * `brand-sheen` (7s), `orbit-pulse` (3.2s), `equalize` (1.15s), `score-radar`
    (3.4s) all run `infinite`. The global prefers-reduced-motion block is
    correct and present — but for everyone else these animate forever, on the
    landing page and on the report, costing battery and attention.
  * No motion tokens: durations are 80ms/150ms/160ms/170ms/180ms/220ms picked
    per-rule, easings are all `ease`.

1.7 PERFORMANCE: the big one is Monaco, and it is eager

  * Monaco is imported statically into the interview route and themed
    `vs-dark`, hard-coded. It is ~1 MB+ of JS and workers, loaded before the
    candidate has even started voice.
  * No route-level code splitting beyond Next's defaults; the decorative
    constellation canvases ship with the marketing route.
  * No budgets, no Lighthouse CI, no bundle check for the web app (the repo has
    `check:bundle` for forbidden content, not for size).

1.8 STATES: the happy path is designed, the rest is text

  * Loading exists for the scenario list (skeletons) and the report (spinner).
    Elsewhere: raw strings ("Loading…", "Reconnecting").
  * No designed empty state for history beyond a bordered box, no designed
    offline state, no microphone-permission-denied state, no session-expired
    state, no 404/500 pages at all.

1.9 SUMMARY JUDGEMENT

The current UI is a competent single-author dark dev-tool skin with real
product thinking inside it and no system underneath it. It will not survive
another five screens, it cannot be themed, it cannot be tested, and the parts
most specific to this product — presence, attention, time, silence — are the
parts it expresses least.

2. DESIGN THESIS AND PRINCIPLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THESIS. This is not a dashboard, an IDE, or a chat app. It is a ROOM. Someone
is sitting across from the candidate, mostly quiet, occasionally asking
something hard. The interface's job is to make that presence believable, keep
the candidate's hands and eyes where they belong — the editor and their own
thinking — and then get out of the way for forty-five minutes.

The marketing surface has the opposite job: convince a nervous person that
this is worth sitting through, with evidence, not adjectives.

PRINCIPLES

  P1. ONE PRIMARY SURFACE PER SCREEN.
      Landing: the promise. Setup: the choice. Interview: the editor.
      Report: the evidence. Everything else is secondary chrome and must look
      secondary. The interview screen currently gives the editor 64% of width
      and the header 8 competing controls; after the redesign the editor is the
      only thing with full contrast.

  P2. SILENCE IS A VISUAL STATE, NOT AN ABSENCE.
      The interviewer being quiet should look deliberate: a steady presence
      marker that reads "listening", not an empty panel. Dead UI reads as a
      dropped call, exactly as dead audio does (that is why room tone exists in
      the voice path — the same argument applies to pixels).

  P3. CALM UNDER STRESS.
      No red countdowns, no shrinking bars, no motion in peripheral vision
      while the candidate codes. Pressure comes from the interview. The UI's
      only job under stress is to be legible.

  P4. EVIDENCE OVER DECORATION.
      Every number shown is traceable to the event log. The report leads with
      what was observed, not with a score ring. Model-estimated grades stay
      labelled as estimates, visually subordinate to evidence.

  P5. EVERY STATE IS DESIGNED.
      For each surface: loading, empty, partial, error, offline, permission
      denied, expired, ended. A state without a design is a bug with a
      stylesheet.

  P6. ACCESSIBLE BY CONSTRUCTION, NOT BY AUDIT.
      Tokens that cannot fail contrast, primitives that cannot ship without a
      focus ring, a single announcement channel, keyboard-first flows. The
      audit then confirms rather than discovers.

  P7. THE SYSTEM IS SMALL AND CLOSED.
      ~16 primitives, ~14 product components, 3 layout shells. If a screen
      needs something new, it goes into the system or it does not ship.

  P8. HONEST SURFACES.
      Beta is labelled beta. Estimates are labelled estimates. Deletion says
      what it deletes. Nothing is pre-consented.

3. DESIGN LANGUAGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 COLOUR

Model: OKLCH ramps, authored once, exported as tokens. OKLCH because equal
lightness steps look equal across hues, which is what makes a 12-step ramp
usable for both themes without hand-fixing every swatch. Baseline-safe in 2026.

Ramps (12 steps each, L from 0.98 to 0.18):
  neutral   C 0.012, H 255   surfaces, borders, text
  accent    C 0.14,  H 35    the single brand accent (warm coral)
  info      C 0.12,  H 255   links, selection, "speaking"
  success   C 0.11,  H 160   connected, ready, "listening"
  warning   C 0.12,  H 85    reconnecting, degraded
  danger    C 0.15,  H 25    destructive, failure

Semantic layer — components NEVER reference a ramp step directly:

  surface.base            page background
  surface.sunken          wells, terminal, editor gutter
  surface.raised          cards, panels
  surface.overlay         dialogs, popovers
  border.subtle / strong / focus
  text.primary / secondary / tertiary / inverted / link
  state.listening / speaking / waiting / offline / recording
  intent.accent / info / success / warning / danger  (+ .surface, .border,
                                                      .text variants)

Theming: one token set, two value sets, resolved with `light-dark()`, with
`color-scheme: light dark` on :root and an explicit [data-theme] override for
the user's choice (system / light / dark, persisted). Light theme is NOT an
inversion: surfaces are warm off-white (L 0.98 C 0.004 H 90), borders carry
slightly more chroma, shadows are replaced by borders.

Contrast rules, enforced by a token test (section 12):
  * text.primary on any surface: >= 7:1   (AAA body text; this app is read
    under stress, by tired people, often on bad laptop panels)
  * text.secondary: >= 4.5:1
  * text.tertiary: >= 4.5:1 at >= 14px; never used below 14px
  * borders and non-text state indicators: >= 3:1 against their background
  * focus ring: >= 3:1 against BOTH the component and the adjacent background
  * No state is communicated by colour alone — every state colour is paired
    with a glyph shape or a word.

3.2 TYPOGRAPHY

  Sans:  Inter Variable, self-hosted through next/font/local, subset latin,
         `display: swap`, preloaded on every route. (CSP already allows
         font-src 'self'; no third-party request, no layout shift.)
  Mono:  JetBrains Mono Variable, self-hosted, used for code, the timer,
         identifiers, and data — never for UI labels.

Scale (clamp-based, 1.25 ratio above body, fixed below):
  display   clamp(40px, 5.2vw, 64px)   line 1.02  tracking -0.03em
  title     clamp(28px, 3.2vw, 40px)   line 1.1   tracking -0.02em
  heading   24px / 1.25
  subhead   19px / 1.35
  body      16px / 1.6     <- new base, up from 14px
  small     14px / 1.5
  caption   13px / 1.4     <- HARD FLOOR. Nothing in the product is smaller.
  code      14px / 1.65 mono, tabular figures on
  timer     28px mono, `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`

Rules:
  * Uppercase labels keep 0.08em tracking and are never below 13px.
  * Measure: 60-75ch for prose, enforced with `max-inline-size: 68ch`.
  * `text-wrap: balance` on headings, `pretty` on paragraphs.
  * One weight axis in use: 400 / 500 / 600 / 700. No 680/720/760 oddities
    (today's file uses all three, which the fallback font cannot render).

3.3 SPACE, SHAPE, ELEVATION

  Space scale (4px base): 2 4 8 12 16 20 24 32 40 56 72 96
  Radii: xs 6, sm 8, md 12, lg 16, xl 24, pill 999
  Borders: 1px hairline everywhere; 2px only for focus.
  Elevation: exactly three levels.
     e0 flat (border only)
     e1 cards/popover   0 1px 2px / 0 8px 24px  at 12-18% alpha
     e2 dialogs         0 24px 64px at 40% alpha + 1px border.strong
     In light theme e1/e2 lose 60% of their alpha and gain border contrast.
  Containers: prose 720px, app 1200px, wide 1440px, full bleed for the cockpit.

3.4 MOTION

  Tokens:
    duration.instant 80ms   duration.fast 140ms   duration.base 220ms
    duration.slow 360ms     duration.deliberate 600ms
    ease.standard cubic-bezier(.2,0,0,1)
    ease.enter    cubic-bezier(0,0,0,1)
    ease.exit     cubic-bezier(.3,0,1,1)

  Rules:
    * Interface motion only moves things that changed, and only on enter/exit,
      hover/press, and value changes.
    * NOTHING animates infinitely on the interview screen except the voice
      presence indicator, and only while the interviewer is actually speaking.
    * The landing hero may carry one ambient motion; it pauses when the tab is
      hidden and stops entirely at `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`.
    * View Transitions (progressive enhancement) for route changes between
      history <-> report and setup -> interview; wrapped in @supports and
      skipped under reduced motion.
    * Reduced motion is a first-class theme, not a kill switch: transitions
      become 1ms, but state changes still cross-fade opacity so nothing
      teleports.

3.5 BRAND AND ICONOGRAPHY

  * The monogram stays, redrawn as a 24px SVG with a 1px keyline, no gradient
    sheen, no infinite shine animation.
  * Icon set: a small hand-built SVG sprite (24x24, 1.5px stroke, round caps):
    mic, mic-off, speaker, speaker-off, play, stop, check, alert, info, clock,
    code, notes, captions, settings, history, trash, download, external,
    chevron, close, drag-handle. ~21 icons, one sprite file, ~6 KB.
  * No emoji or dingbats as UI glyphs. Today's "⌁", "→", "✓" in content strings
    are replaced by icons with proper `aria-hidden` and text alternatives.
  * Illustration: none. The product's visual identity is typography, space, and
    one accent. Illustration would make an interview room look like a toy.

4. TOKEN ARCHITECTURE, STYLESHEET STRATEGY, THEMING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 SOURCE OF TRUTH

  design/tokens/*.json in DTCG format (the W3C Design Tokens community-group
  format; `$type`/`$value`, colour expressed in OKLCH components):

    core.json        ramps, space, radii, type, duration, easing
    semantic.json    surface/text/border/state/intent, light + dark values
    component.json   per-component overrides that must not leak (e.g.
                     editor.gutter, caption.interim)

  Build step: `pnpm tokens` runs Style Dictionary and emits
    apps/web/app/styles/tokens.css       (CSS custom properties, both themes)
    apps/web/lib/tokens.ts               (typed subset for canvas/Monaco)
    apps/web/lib/monaco-theme.ts         (generated Monaco theme from tokens)
  Generated files are committed, so the app never depends on the build step at
  runtime and reviewers can see colour diffs.

4.2 STYLESHEET STRATEGY

  Decision: CSS Modules per component + a small global layer. No Tailwind, no
  runtime CSS-in-JS.
  Why: React 19 server components make runtime CSS-in-JS a liability; Tailwind
  would rewrite every file in this repo for a codebase that has exactly one
  author and a strong comment culture; CSS Modules keep the existing authoring
  style, give scoping for free, and split per route automatically in Next 15.

  Cascade layers, declared once in app/styles/index.css:

    @layer reset, tokens, base, layout, components, utilities, overrides;

  Files:
    app/styles/index.css      layer order + imports (the ONLY global import)
    app/styles/reset.css      modern reset (box-sizing, margin, media defaults)
    app/styles/tokens.css     generated
    app/styles/base.css       element defaults, typography, focus, selection
    app/styles/utilities.css  ~12 utilities only (visually-hidden, stack,
                              cluster, grid-auto, truncate, scroll-area)
  Every component ships `Component.module.css` next to it.
  globals.css is deleted at the end of phase 4, not before.

4.3 THEMING

  * `:root { color-scheme: light dark }` and every semantic token defined with
    `light-dark(<light>, <dark>)`.
  * `<html data-theme="system|light|dark">` set by a tiny inline script in the
    root layout (before paint, no flash), persisted in localStorage, defaulting
    to system.
  * Monaco theme is generated from the same tokens for both themes and switched
    with the app theme (today it is hard-coded `vs-dark`).
  * `@media (forced-colors: active)`: borders become `CanvasText`, state dots
    gain glyphs, shadows are dropped, focus uses `Highlight`.

5. COMPONENT SYSTEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 PRIMITIVES (apps/web/components/ui/)

  Button         variants: primary | secondary | ghost | danger
                 sizes: sm (32px) | md (40px) | lg (48px)
                 states: idle, hover, active, focus-visible, disabled, loading
                 loading replaces the label with a spinner AND keeps width
  IconButton     square, min 40x40 hit area even at 24px icon (SC 2.5.8)
  Link           underline on hover, visible focus ring, external marker
  Field          label + control + hint + error, wires aria-describedby and
                 aria-invalid automatically; errors announced once
  Input / Textarea / Select / Checkbox / Radio / RadioCard / Switch
  Dialog         native <dialog>, focus trap, Escape, scroll lock, labelled by
                 its heading; replaces the two hand-rolled modals
  Popover        anchor-positioned with @supports fallback to absolute
  Tooltip        hover + focus, never the only source of information
  Toast          one region, polite, max 3, auto-dismiss 6s, pause on hover
  Panel          titled region with optional actions; the cockpit's unit
  Card           elevation e1 surface with optional header/footer
  Badge / Pill   status colour + glyph + text
  Meter          labelled bar with accessible value text
  Skeleton       shimmer honours reduced motion (becomes a static block)
  Tabs           roving tabindex, arrow keys
  Stat           value + label + optional trend, tabular figures

  DimensionCard          rubric dimension + meter + evidence rows.

5.3 LAYOUT SHELLS

  MarketingShell   max 1200px, nav + footer, prose containers inside
  AppShell         authenticated pages: compact nav, account menu, content
  CockpitShell     full-bleed, no page scroll, grid with resizable split

5.4 CONVENTIONS

  * Props over classNames. A component exposes variants, never `className`
    for structural overrides (a `className` escape hatch is allowed only for
    margin/positioning, via a documented `spacing` prop instead).
  * Every interactive component accepts and forwards `ref`, `id`,
    `aria-*`, and `data-testid`.
  * Server components by default; `"use client"` only where there is state,
    an event handler, or a browser API. Today the whole landing page is a
    client component because of one form; after the split, only the setup
    island is.
  * No component may define a colour literal. CI greps for `#` and `rgba(` in
    `*.module.css` and fails the build (tokens.css is the only exception).

6. SCREEN BY SCREEN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Each screen below lists: JOB (what one thing it must do), LAYOUT (desktop /
tablet / phone), WHAT CHANGES, STATES, and A11Y NOTES.

6.1 GLOBAL SHELL

  JOB: disappear.
  LAYOUT: 56px nav. Left: brand. Right: theme toggle, history, account. The
  nav is `position: sticky` only on marketing pages; app pages pin it.
  WHAT CHANGES:
    * Root layout renders a real `<main id="main-content">`; pages render
      `<section>`s inside it. The skip link finally targets a landmark.
    * Footer: three link groups, a one-line honest beta notice, no social
      icons, no newsletter.
    * `lang`, `dir`, viewport-fit=cover, and a theme-color meta per theme.
  STATES: signed out (Sign in), signed in (account menu), offline (a single
  thin status strip under the nav, never a modal).
  A11Y: one `<nav aria-label="Main">`, one `<footer>`, landmarks unique.

6.2 LANDING (/)

  JOB: make a nervous candidate believe this is a real interview, in 15
  seconds, with evidence.

  LAYOUT (desktop): a 12-column grid, 1200px.
    1. Hero, 7 cols copy + 5 cols "the room" visual.
       H1 (display), one paragraph (body, 60ch), two actions: primary
       "Start an interview", secondary "See a sample report".
       Three proof lines with icons: original problems, 45 minutes,
       evidence-backed report. No vanity metrics, no logos we do not have.
    2. "What happens in the room" — a 3-step horizontal flow (Hear the
       problem / Think out loud / Get the evidence). Each step is text plus
       one small, honest visual: a waveform at rest, a cursor in an editor, a
       report row with a quote. Replaces the current fake console mock.
    3. "What makes it hard in the right way" — 4 cards of product truth:
       the interviewer stays silent while you work; it only says what a human
       author wrote; no autocomplete; your transcript is yours and deletable.
       This is the differentiator section and it is text-led.
    4. Sample evidence block — a real (anonymised) report fragment. The single
       most persuasive element on the page; nothing else proves the output.
    5. FAQ teaser (4 questions, link to /faq) + footer.
  LAYOUT (tablet): hero stacks, flow becomes 3 rows, cards 2x2.
  LAYOUT (phone): single column, actions full width, visual dropped below the
  fold, proof lines become a list.

  WHAT CHANGES:
    * The setup form LEAVES the landing page. Today the landing page is the
      marketing page, the resume-consent form, the mode picker, and the
      scenario list in one 370-line client component. Split: marketing is a
      server component; setup moves to /start (6.4).
    * The decorative constellation canvas is removed from the marketing route
      entirely (it costs JS for texture); the hero texture becomes a static
      CSS mask + one cheap, pausable animation.
    * The fake "live interview preview" console is replaced by honest
      artefacts: a still editor frame and a real report row.
  STATES: default; reduced motion (ambient motion off); offline (actions
  disabled with explanation).
  A11Y: one h1; section headings in order; the hero visual is decorative and
  `aria-hidden`; CTA buttons are links styled as buttons (they navigate).

6.3 SIGN IN (/login)

  JOB: get an email code entered with zero friction, zero cognitive tests.
  LAYOUT: single 420px card, centred, 2 steps (email -> 6-digit code).
  WHAT CHANGES:
    * Code input becomes a single `inputmode="numeric"`,
      `autocomplete="one-time-code"` field (not 6 boxes — 6 boxes break paste
      and screen readers, and SC 3.3.8 wants pasting to work).
    * Email is remembered between steps and on retry (SC 3.3.7 Redundant
      Entry).
    * A "resend code" with a visible 30s cooldown and a live countdown.
    * A persistent, identical "Need help?" link on both steps (SC 3.2.6).
  STATES: idle, sending, sent, invalid code, expired code, rate limited,
  network failure. Each has copy written in section 10's voice.
  A11Y: errors are `role="alert"` on the field, announced once; the step
  change moves focus to the new step's heading.

6.4 START / SETUP (/start) — NEW ROUTE

  JOB: choose a room and begin, in under a minute, without feeling tested
  before the test.

  LAYOUT: a 3-step flow with a persistent summary rail (desktop) or a sticky
  bottom bar (phone).
    Step 1 — Session. Scenario radio cards (level, topics, 45 min) +
             "Surprise me" (the account-unseen default).
    Step 2 — Room. Mode (Learning / Mock / Strict) as radio cards, each with
             one consequence sentence ("Strict waits longer before it speaks
             and gives one hint at most"). Tone (Extra nice / Normal / Mean)
             as a second row with the same treatment.
    Step 3 — Context (optional). Resume paste, consent checkbox (never
             pre-ticked), extracted-fact review with per-fact checkboxes and
             an explicit "Erase source text" control.
  A right-hand summary rail shows the four choices and the only primary
  action: "Enter interview". The action is never duplicated.

  WHAT CHANGES: this is an entirely new screen assembled from existing logic;
  no server contract changes. The current single-page setup becomes three
  short, reversible steps with a visible summary, which is how people actually
  make a choice they are anxious about.
  STATES: scenarios loading (3 skeleton cards), scenario list empty, resume
  analysis running (inline, cancellable), analysis failed (continue without),
  admission blocked (capacity), creating session (button loading, 10s timeout
  with a retry that is idempotent).
  A11Y: each step is a `<fieldset>` with a `<legend>`; radio cards are real
  radios with `RadioCard`; the summary rail is `aria-live="polite"` and
  announces only the final changed value.

6.5 VOICE PREFLIGHT (dialog, before the first interviewer word)

  JOB: prove the microphone and speaker work before the clock matters.
  LAYOUT: dialog, 560px, three checks as a vertical checklist:
    1. Microphone permission -> device select + live level meter
    2. Output device -> "Play test tone" (room tone, 2s)
    3. Connection -> a single latency reading from the voice-ready probe
  Primary: "Start the interview". Secondary: "Skip checks".
  STATES: idle, checking, ready, denied (with the exact browser steps for
  Chrome/Safari/Firefox), no device, insecure context, already in use.
  A11Y: the level meter exposes `role="meter"` with value text ("input level
  62 percent"); nothing relies on the animated bar alone; the dialog is
  dismissible and never traps a user who cannot pass a check.

6.6 THE INTERVIEW COCKPIT (/interview/[sessionId]) — the centrepiece

  JOB: the candidate codes and talks. Everything else is peripheral.

  LAYOUT (desktop >= 1100px):
    Row 1  Cockpit bar, 56px, three zones:
             left   brand mark (24px), session stage ("Implementation"),
                    connection chip (dot + word)
             centre StageRail (7 stages, current named)
             right  PresenceIndicator, TimerDial, overflow menu (Report a
                    problem, Settings, Complete interview)
           "Complete interview" leaves the bar and lives in the overflow menu
           with a confirm dialog — ending a session is a decision, not a CTA,
           and a 40px red button next to a timer invites misclicks.
    Row 2  Workspace, a resizable 2-pane split (drag handle, double-click to
           reset, persisted per user; keyboard-resizable with arrow keys when
           the handle is focused — and a non-drag alternative in the view menu
           per SC 2.5.7).
             left   CodeSurface (Monaco), full height, its own panel header
                    (file name, language, save state, font size)
             right  a stack of two panels: NotesSurface and CaptionStream,
                    each collapsible to a 36px header. Collapsing is how a
                    candidate gets a full-width editor without leaving.
    Row 3  (only when the runner is enabled) RunPanel as a bottom drawer,
           collapsed by default, opens to 240px.

  LAYOUT (tablet 700-1100px): the right stack becomes a tab bar (Notes /
  Captions) above the editor; the editor keeps >= 60% of height.
  LAYOUT (phone): single column; a 3-tab bottom bar (Code / Notes / Captions);
  the cockpit bar collapses to brand + timer + presence; stage moves into a
  sheet behind the stage chip. `100dvh` with `env(safe-area-inset-*)`.

  THE PRESENCE MODEL (this is the heart of the redesign):
    * One orb, 28px, three states, each with a distinct SHAPE as well as a
      colour: listening = three steady bars; waiting = one dim dot; speaking =
      three bars moving at the audio's own envelope.
    * When the interviewer speaks, the editor pane dims by 4% and the orb is
      the only moving thing on screen. When it stops, the dim lifts over
      220ms. That is the entire "someone is talking to you" affordance: no
      banner, no transcript, no modal.
    * Barge-in (candidate talks over the interviewer) shows the orb return to
      "listening" immediately — the UI confirms the yield the audio already
      made.
    * While the candidate speaks, a hairline at the top of the caption panel
      pulses with their own level. This is the only place candidate audio is
      visualised, and it is where their eyes already are when they check that
      they are being heard.

  WHAT CHANGES vs today:
    * The header loses 3 of its 8 controls to an overflow menu.
    * Captions get a real panel with its own scroll, a "listening/paused"
      chip, and an interim line that is visually distinct (italic + 60%
      opacity) instead of a muted paragraph.
    * The editor gets a panel header with save state (it has one today) plus
      font-size control and a collapse toggle for the side stack.
    * `.workspace-loading`'s opacity+saturate dim is replaced by skeletons
      inside each panel, so text never drops below contrast.
    * The voice-onboarding strip becomes a proper, dismissible `Panel` with a
      single primary action ("Start voice") and the keyboard hint.
    * All 100vh become 100dvh; the grid uses `min-height: 0` correctly so
      Monaco stops fighting flexbox on Safari.
  STATES: connecting, connected, reconnecting (strip, not modal), offline
  (strip + save queue count), voice idle, voice failed (inline recovery with
  device picker), microphone lost (recovery in place), provider rotating
  (invisible by design — the UI must not blink), ending, ended.
  A11Y:
    * ONE polite live region for the whole screen, owned by a tiny
      announcer store. Presence, connection, save state and caption finals all
      publish into it with priorities; the caption panel itself becomes
      `aria-live="off"` with a "read captions" toggle for screen-reader users
      who want them verbatim. This fixes the double-announce problem.
    * Keyboard map is complete (section 7) and discoverable via "?" overlay.
    * Panel collapse/expand buttons expose `aria-expanded` + `aria-controls`.
    * The split handle is `role="separator"` with `aria-valuenow`.
    * Focus is never obscured by the sticky bar: `scroll-margin-block-start:
      72px` on all focusables inside scroll containers (SC 2.4.11).

6.7 REPORT (/report/[sessionId])

  JOB: show the candidate what actually happened, with quotes, before any
  number.

  LAYOUT:
    1. Header: scenario, mode, date, duration, and a single "Download" menu
       (JSON / Markdown). No hero mesh, no animated ring.
    2. "What happened" — a 5-6 row timeline of the interview's observable
       beats (brief delivered, first approach stated, first code, first run,
       probe asked, follow-up). Each row is a timestamp + one sentence.
       This section is NEW and it is the most useful thing on the page.
    3. Evidence by dimension — DimensionCards, each with a labelled meter, two
       strengths, two gaps, and 2-3 EvidenceRows (timestamp + exact quote).
    4. Model-estimated grades — the two independent graders, visually
       subordinate (secondary surface, smaller type), each with an explicit
       "model estimate, not an executed test" label and its confidence.
    5. "What to practise next" — 3 concrete drills derived from the gaps.
    6. Privacy footer: what was stored, how long, and a delete control.
  WHAT CHANGES: the score ring stops being the first thing a human sees. The
  current page leads with a 150px animated orb carrying a single number;
  that is the one element most likely to be misread as a verdict (I4 applies
  to the live screen, but the spirit applies here: evidence first).
  STATES: generating (progress with the 3 phases and an honest estimate),
  partial (one grader failed — show what exists, say what is missing), failed
  (retry), expired/deleted, not yours (404).
  A11Y: the meters expose text values; quotes are `<blockquote>` with
  `<cite>`; the download menu is a real menu with roving focus.

6.8 HISTORY (/history)

  JOB: resume what is live, open what is done, delete what you regret.
  LAYOUT: list of HistoryRows: scenario + date + duration, status badge
  (Active / Completed / Expiring in N days), actions (Resume / Report /
  Export / Delete). Grouped by month with sticky subheads. Cursor pagination
  with a "Load more" that preserves scroll.
  STATES: loading (3 skeleton rows), empty (EmptyState with "Start your first
  interview"), error, deleting (row-level optimistic state with undo for 5s
  BEFORE the destructive call fires — not after).
  A11Y: the list is a `<ul>`; each row's actions are reachable in tab order
  and labelled with the session name, not "Delete".

6.9 SETTINGS (/settings)

  JOB: make privacy controls obvious and honest.
  LAYOUT: three cards — Account (email, sign out), Practice data (export all,
  delete all practice data), Danger zone (delete account). Theme and caption
  preferences move here too (theme, caption reading mode, editor font size).
  WHAT CHANGES: destructive actions require typed confirmation (they already
  do) and now show exactly what will be removed, as a list with counts, before
  the button is enabled. The receipt after deletion stays, and names anything
  that could not be reached.
  A11Y: the confirmation input is labelled with the exact phrase required;
  the receipt is `role="status"`.

6.10 POLICY, FAQ, SUPPORT

  JOB: be readable. Prose shell, 68ch, h2 sections with anchor links, a table
  of contents on desktop, "last updated" dates that are real. FAQ becomes a
  disclosure list (`<details>` styled, or a controlled accordion with proper
  `aria-expanded`), not a two-column grid that collapses badly.

6.11 ERRORS AND EDGE ROUTES (NEW)

  * app/not-found.tsx — 404 with a search-free, honest message and two links.
  * app/error.tsx — client error boundary with a support reference id and a
    "Report a problem" action that pre-fills the diagnostic category.
  * app/interview/[sessionId]/error.tsx — the one case where the message must
    say "your code and notes are saved" first, because that is the only
    question the candidate has.
  * Offline: a single strip, never a blocking dialog; queued-save count shown.

7. INTERACTION AND KEYBOARD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Global
    ?            keyboard help overlay (dialog listing everything below)
    g then h     history        g then s     settings
    t            cycle theme (system -> light -> dark)
  Cockpit
    Cmd/Ctrl+Enter   run tests (when the runner is enabled)
    Cmd/Ctrl+/       toggle the side stack (full-width editor)
    Cmd/Ctrl+1/2/3   focus editor / notes / captions
    m                toggle microphone mute (not while typing in a field)
    Shift+?          help overlay
    Escape           close dialog, then popover, then overlay (one level per
                     press, never navigates away)
  Rules
    * Every shortcut is listed in the help overlay and nowhere else is it
      required to discover.
    * No single-key shortcut fires while focus is in a text field or Monaco.
    * No drag-only interaction anywhere (SC 2.5.7): the split handle has menu
      items for 50/50, editor-wide and notes-wide.
    * Hit targets >= 40x40 for primary controls, >= 24x24 absolute minimum
      with 8px spacing for dense icon rows (SC 2.5.8).
    * Focus order follows DOM order on every screen; no `tabindex > 0`.
    * `:focus-visible` ring: 2px `border.focus` + 2px offset, on every
      interactive element including links (today's gap), with a 3:1 contrast
      check against both adjacent surfaces.

8. ACCESSIBILITY SPECIFICATION (WCAG 2.2 AA, with the AAA we choose to take)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Target: WCAG 2.2 Level AA across the product, plus AAA body-text contrast
  (7:1) and AAA focus appearance (2.4.13) because this app is used under
  stress on bad screens.

  The 2.2-specific criteria and how this design satisfies them:
    2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (AA)   sticky bars use scroll-margin and never
                                     overlay the focused element
    2.5.7  Dragging Movements (AA)   split handle has non-drag equivalents
    2.5.8  Target Size (AA)          40px primary, 24px floor with spacing
    3.2.6  Consistent Help (A)       "Report a problem" and "Help" appear in
                                     the same relative position on every
                                     authenticated screen
    3.3.7  Redundant Entry (A)       email persisted across sign-in steps;
                                     setup choices persisted across the 3
                                     steps and across a reload
    3.3.8  Accessible Authentication (AA)  email code only; paste allowed; no
                                     puzzle, no cognitive test, no CAPTCHA

  Live regions and announcements (the hardest part of this app):
    * Exactly ONE `aria-live="polite"` announcer element at the app root.
    * A tiny `announce(message, {priority})` store; components never render
      their own live regions. Priorities: interviewer-speaking > connection >
      save-state > caption-final.
    * Captions default to `aria-live="off"` (they are visual redundancy of the
      user's own speech, and reading them back is noise for a screen-reader
      user); a Settings toggle turns verbatim caption announcement on.
    * `role="alert"` is reserved for failures that stop the interview.

  Screen reader script for the interview (what a blind candidate hears):
    "Interview, implementation stage. Connected. 38 minutes 12 seconds
     remaining." ... "Interviewer speaking." ... "Interviewer listening."
     ... "All changes saved."
    Nothing else is announced automatically.

  Other requirements:
    * 400% zoom / 320px-equivalent reflow on every page except the cockpit,
      which reflows to its phone layout at that scale.
    * `prefers-contrast: more` increases border contrast one step.
    * `forced-colors: active` audited on every screen.
    * All icons `aria-hidden` with adjacent text or `aria-label` on the
      control.
    * Captions, timer and presence are never the sole carrier of a critical
      message.
    * Motion: everything above section 3.4's rules, plus no parallax, no
      auto-playing video, ever.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. PERFORMANCE AND ENGINEERING BUDGET
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Field targets (Core Web Vitals, 75th percentile):
    LCP  <= 2.0s   (marketing), <= 2.5s (app)
    INP  <= 200ms  everywhere, measured on the cockpit under a live session
    CLS  <= 0.05

  Bundle budgets, enforced in CI:
    marketing route   <= 110 KB JS gzipped (today: the landing page is a
                      client component plus two canvas components)
    app routes        <= 170 KB JS gzipped excluding Monaco
    Monaco            lazy, imported on interaction or on idle after the
                      cockpit's first paint, never in the initial chunk
    CSS               <= 45 KB gzipped total, split per route by CSS Modules

  Techniques:
    * Landing, FAQ, privacy, support become server components; the only
      client islands are the theme toggle and the setup form.
    * `next/font/local` for both typefaces, `preload` on the two weights used
      above the fold, `size-adjust` fallback metrics to kill font CLS.
    * Monaco: `dynamic(() => import(...), { ssr: false, loading: <Skeleton/> })`
      plus a `requestIdleCallback` prefetch once the cockpit is interactive.
      Workers already come from our own origin (CSP stays untouched).
    * The constellation canvases are deleted from the marketing bundle; if the
      texture survives at all it becomes a static SVG/CSS mask.
    * Images: the OG image stays generated; no raster assets anywhere else.
    * Prefetch `/start` from the landing CTA, `/report/[id]` from a completed
      history row.
    * Zero layout shift from the timer (tabular figures) and from status chips
      (fixed min-width).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. CONTENT, MICROCOPY AND TONE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Voice: calm, concrete, never congratulatory. The interviewer persona is
  "neutral and unhurried"; the interface matches it.

  Rules:
    * Say what happened, then what to do. "Your microphone disconnected. Pick
      another device — your code, notes and time are safe."
    * Never praise the user ("Great job!"), never apologise theatrically
      ("Oops!"), never use exclamation marks.
    * Numbers that are estimates say so, every time they appear.
    * Destructive copy names the object and the scope: "Delete this interview
      and its report" not "Are you sure?".
    * Empty states state the next action, not the absence: "Start your first
      interview" not "Nothing here yet".
    * Error messages carry a support reference when one exists.
    * Buttons are verbs in the user's words: "Enter interview", "Complete
      interview", "Send diagnostic report".
    * Nothing in the UI may describe the interviewer's internal reasoning (I6)
      or imply a live grade (I4).

11. IMPLEMENTATION PLAN (phased, each phase independently shippable)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  P0 — Foundations (no visual change)                         ~1 day
       design/tokens/*.json, `pnpm tokens`, tokens.css generated,
       app/styles/{index,reset,base,utilities}.css, @layer order declared,
       globals.css imported LAST inside @layer overrides so nothing moves yet.
       next/font wired for Inter + JetBrains Mono.
       Exit: build green, screenshots byte-identical except font rendering.

  P1 — Primitives                                             ~2 days
       components/ui/* with CSS Modules + unit tests + a /dev/styleguide route
       (dev-only, excluded from the sitemap and robots) rendering every
       primitive in every state, both themes, at 3 widths.
       Exit: styleguide renders; axe clean; no colour literals outside tokens.

  P2 — Theming and the global shell                           ~1 day
       light-dark() semantic tokens, data-theme script, ThemeToggle,
       Monaco theme generated, root layout landmarks fixed, forced-colors pass.
       Exit: both themes pass contrast tests; no flash on load.

  P3 — The cockpit                                            ~3 days
       CockpitShell, cockpit bar, PresenceIndicator, TimerDial, StageRail,
       CaptionStream, CodeSurface (lazy Monaco), NotesSurface, resizable
       split, single announcer, dvh/safe-area, all states.
       Exit: keyboard-only interview completable end to end; INP <= 200ms with
       a live session; screen-reader script matches 8.

  P4 — Setup, preflight, sign-in                              ~2 days
       /start 3-step flow, VoicePreflightDialog, login rework, redundant-entry
       persistence. Landing becomes a server component and loses the form.
       Exit: no client JS on / except two islands; flows pass axe + keyboard.

  P5 — Report, history, settings, policy, error routes        ~2 days
       Timeline section, evidence-first order, subordinate grades, history
       grouping + undo, settings deletion preview, 404/500/offline.
       Exit: report renders every partial/failed state from fixtures.

  P6 — Landing and brand                                      ~1.5 days
       New hero, three-step flow, truth cards, sample evidence block, footer,
       OG image refresh, icon sprite, monogram.
       Exit: LCP <= 2.0s on a throttled run; bundle under budget.

  P7 — Deletion                                               ~0.5 day
       Delete globals.css, the constellation components, and every dead
       selector. CI check: no file outside app/styles may contain a colour.
       Exit: CSS total under 45 KB gzipped; visual snapshots unchanged.

  Order rationale: tokens before components, components before screens, the
  cockpit before everything else because it is where the product lives and
  where a regression costs a real interview. Marketing last because it is the
  only surface that can ship visibly unfinished without hurting a user.

  Migration strategy: strangler. globals.css survives inside `@layer overrides`
  until P7, so a half-migrated screen is never broken — the old rules keep
  winning until the new component owns the markup.

12. VERIFICATION AND QA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Automated, in CI, blocking:
    * `pnpm tokens:check` — regenerates tokens and fails on drift.
    * Contrast test — every semantic pairing in both themes computed against
      its rule in 3.1; fails the build on a violation. This is why tokens
      cannot fail contrast: a human never eyeballs a hex again.
    * axe-core via Playwright on 9 routes x 2 themes x (default + reduced
      motion). Zero violations allowed; exceptions require a comment with the
      criterion number.
    * Playwright visual snapshots at 390 / 834 / 1440 px, both themes.
    * Keyboard-only script: start an interview, type, mute, open help, end,
      read the report — asserted by focus order, not by pixels.
    * Lighthouse CI with the budgets in section 9.
    * Bundle size check per route.

  Manual, before each phase merges:
    * NVDA + Chrome and VoiceOver + Safari through the cockpit script in 8.
    * 400% zoom on every screen.
    * Windows High Contrast on the cockpit and the report.
    * One full interview on a 13" laptop at 100% and at 125% OS scaling.

  Definition of done for the redesign as a whole:
    * Every route renders every state from a fixture, in both themes.
    * No `#` or `rgba(` outside tokens.css.
    * globals.css deleted.
    * All six acceptance checks in section 11's exits pass.
    * The product invariants in section 0 are visibly intact.

13. ANTI-GOALS (things a redesign of this product must refuse)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * A chat transcript of the interviewer, "so you can scroll back". It turns a
    listening exercise into a reading exercise and leaks the brief as text.
  * An avatar, a face, a name, a personality illustration.
  * Gamification: streaks, XP, badges, "you beat 72% of candidates".
  * A live score, a confidence meter, or any hint of the gate's reasoning.
  * Urgency theatre: red timers, heartbeat animation, "2 minutes left!".
  * AI autocomplete, inline hints, or a "stuck?" helper in the editor.
  * Modal interruptions during a live interview for anything but a failure
    that ends it.
  * A cookie banner we do not need, or any pre-ticked consent.
  * Infinite ambient animation on any screen where someone is working.
  * A component library dependency that owns our look (shadcn, MUI, Chakra).
    The system here is small enough that owning it costs less than theming
    someone else's.

14. OPEN QUESTIONS FOR THE OWNER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Q1. Brand: keep the coral accent, or move to a cooler, more clinical palette
      for the interview room and reserve warmth for marketing? The plan keeps
      coral; one accent is enough either way.
  Q2. Is /start as a separate route acceptable, or must setup stay on the
      landing page for conversion reasons? (Recommendation: separate. The
      landing page's job and the setup form's job are different, and the form
      is what forces the whole marketing page to be a client component.)
  Q3. Light theme at launch, or dark-only until after the pilot? (The plan
      builds both from tokens, so light costs about half a day extra.)
  Q4. Does the report keep a single headline number at all? The plan demotes
      it below the timeline and the evidence.
  Q5. Resizable split and panel collapse are persisted per user — is a
      server-side preference acceptable, or should it stay in localStorage?
